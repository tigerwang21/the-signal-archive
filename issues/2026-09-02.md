# From Adoption to Governance

#### Two-in-five UK universities have no AI policy students or regulators can find

A study of 96 institutions finds responses remain assessment-centred and integrity-first rather than strategic. Stirling, Canterbury Christ Church, Arts University Plymouth and Durham are flagged as exemplars worth borrowing from.

[descriptionSOURCE: HEPI ↗](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/what-uk-university-ai-policies-actually-do-a-study-of-96-institutions/)

#### Governors are adding AI to risk registers — but few have a forward strategy

Every governor Advance HE interviewed said AI now sits on their university's risk register, with audit and risk committees reporting to the board. Board-level strategy for AI remains the exception, not the norm.

[descriptionSOURCE: ADVANCE HE ↗](https://advance-he.org/knowledge-hub/advance-he-governor-view-ai-and-governance/)

#### UK universities sign a joint statement on the AI for Science Strategy

Bristol, King's, UCL, Liverpool and Coventry are among signatories committing to a whole-ecosystem approach — interdisciplinary research culture, technical careers and data standards — as DSIT moves to delivery.

[descriptionSOURCE: GOV.UK / DSIT ↗](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-for-science-strategy-uk-universities-joint-statement/ai-for-science-strategy-uk-universities-joint-statement)

#### England's regulator is explicitly pro-innovation on AI

The OfS takes a principles-based approach: no required scale or form of AI use. Its one hard expectation is that guidance be "clear, accessible and well understood" — students' number-one ask.

[descriptionSOURCE: OFFICE FOR STUDENTS ↗](https://www.officeforstudents.org.uk/news-blog-and-events/blog/embracing-innovation-in-higher-education-our-approach-to-artificial-intelligence/)

#### OfS and Advance HE are mapping how institutions actually use AI

A joint research project — survey plus roundtables — runs to July 2026 and will publish findings later this year to help leaders strengthen AI capability and mitigate risk.

[descriptionSOURCE: OFFICE FOR STUDENTS ↗](https://www.officeforstudents.org.uk/news-blog-and-events/press-and-media/ofs-collaborates-with-advance-he-to-conduct-research-into-how-universities-and-colleges-are-using-artificial-intelligence/)

#### Policies are assessment-centred and integrity-first — not pedagogical

A mapping of 132 policy documents across 76 universities finds guidelines concentrate on assessment integrity and misconduct, with far less on integrating AI into teaching itself. The sector remains reactive.

[descriptionSOURCE: UNIVERSITY OF LIVERPOOL ↗](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3199015/1/v1_covered_dda09507-dddb-403e-bf6c-acb850ad1569.pdf)

#### The £490bn prize hinges on graduate skills

Expanding graduate skills in the eight growth sectors and integrating AI could unlock £490bn for the UK by 2030 — but 80% of critical Industrial Strategy occupations require degree-level qualifications the pipeline is not yet delivering.

[descriptionSOURCE: QS / UNIVERSITY OF YORK ↗](https://insights.qs.com/hubfs/Reports/The_Strategic_Role_of_HE_to_deliver_the_National_Industrial_Strategy.pdf)

#### Only 14% of UK learners consider themselves highly AI-ready

43% of employers cite the gap between AI knowledge and real-world application as a key hiring difficulty. Graduates need proof they can apply AI, not just exposure to it.

[descriptionSOURCE: PEARSON / AWS ↗](https://finviz.com/news/370831/new-pearson-and-aws-research-only-14-of-uk-learners-consider-themselves-highly-ai-ready)

#### AI talent is concentrating in London and the South East

The Midlands, North, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland face acute AI skills shortages, threatening to entrench regional inequality and waste dispersed potential.

[descriptionSOURCE: CAIO WEEKLY ↗](https://caioweekly.co.uk/uk-skills-crisis-threatens-490bn-ai-growth-opportunity)

#### The UK has no AI Act — and that is deliberate

The pro-innovation approach routes five cross-cutting principles through existing regulators: ICO for data, FCA for finance, MHRA for medical devices, Ofcom for online safety. Universities answer to sector regulators, not a single AI statute.

[descriptionSOURCE: GLACIS / DSIT ↗](https://www.glacis.io/guide-uk-ai-regulation)

#### Automated decision-making rules changed on 5 February 2026

The Data (Use and Access) Act replaced GDPR Article 22 with Articles 22A–22D: significant automated decisions — including admissions and assessment-adjacent processes — are now permitted subject to safeguards on information, representation, human intervention and contestability.

[descriptionSOURCE: ICO / UK GDPR ↗](https://getregula.com/regions/uk-ai-regulation.html)

#### The AI Growth Lab and ICO guidance are the next milestones

The AI Growth Lab launched in June 2026 as a cross-regulator advisory service; final ICO automated-decision-making and profiling guidance is due winter 2026.

[descriptionSOURCE: GLACIS / ICO ↗](https://www.glacis.io/guide-uk-ai-regulation)
