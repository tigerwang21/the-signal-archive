# Universal Access Arrives — Governance Must Catch Up — Week of September 7, 2026

## Executive brief
UK higher education crossed a procurement threshold this week: campus-wide enterprise AI became the default, and a genuine two-vendor market emerged. Manchester (65,000 staff and students) and Leicester made Microsoft 365 Copilot universal, while Roehampton became the first UK institution to roll out Google Gemini for Education at scale. Oxford's BOLD lab launched a national, open-science AI research model, and Imperial formalised AI governance through a dean-chaired committee. On teaching, Manchester's four-category assessment policy took effect and HEPI confirmed 94% of students now use AI in assessed work — the centre of gravity has decisively shifted from detection to assessment redesign. On compliance, the EU's transparency duties are enforceable now, and a statutory ICO code on AI and automated decision-making is incoming. The through-line for leadership: procurement has outrun governance, and the highest-ROI moves this autumn are assessment redesign, staff enablement and formal guardrails — not more tools.

## Key metrics
- 94% — UK undergraduates using generative AI in assessed work; 12% insert AI-generated text directly (HEPI)
- 65,000 — staff and students given universal Microsoft 365 Copilot access at Manchester, a world-first
- 600+ — Chinese universities now running AI undergraduate programmes, up from 35 in 2018
- 37% — UK AI expert roles requiring a PhD, concentrating skills in a narrow workforce (DSIT)

## AI Strategy & Governance
- **Campus-wide Copilot access becomes the UK default as Manchester and Leicester go universal.** Manchester's "world-first" partnership gives all 65,000 students and staff full Copilot access and training (rollout by summer 2026); Leicester, a first "Microsoft Frontier University," follows with all-community access from September. *Strategic implication:* universal single-vendor access is now the competitive benchmark — the real decision for strategy directors is vendor consolidation, switching costs and data governance, not whether to adopt a tool. ([Manchester](https://www.manchester.ac.uk/about/news/world-first-ai-partnership-between-the-university-of-manchester-and-microsoft-announced/), [Leicester](https://ukstories.microsoft.com/features/microsoft-collaboration-puts-university-of-leicester-at-the-forefront-of-ai-in-education/))
- **Roehampton picks Google Gemini for Education — a second vendor lands at scale.** Every student and staff member gains governed access to Gemini for Education and Gemini Notebook, integrated with Moodle and under enterprise controls where customer data is not used to train foundation models. *Strategic implication:* a genuine two-vendor market is now forming — institutions have a real choice and must weigh ecosystem lock-in, data-residency terms and learning-tool integration rather than defaulting to one ecosystem. ([Study in UK](https://study-in-uk.com/blog/university-of-roehampton-introduces-google-gemini-for-education-to-strengthen-student-success-and-career-readiness/))
- **Oxford-led BOLD lab launches a national open-science AI research operating model.** The British Open-ended Learning and Discovery Lab (launched 2 September) unites five Oxford/UCL/Imperial groups into one shared-infrastructure lab with NVIDIA/AMD/UKRI backing, explicitly to move beyond single-PI fragmentation. *Strategic implication:* frontier AI research is reorganising into pooled, industrially-paced labs — research-intensive institutions must decide whether to pool into shared labs with pre-negotiated IP and compute, rather than fund more isolated groups. ([Oxford Engineering](https://eng.ox.ac.uk/news/british-open-ended-learning-and-discovery-lab-festival-launches))
- **China cuts 12,200 university programmes and reallocates them toward AI-related degrees.** Under the state "AI + Education" plan, 600+ universities now run AI undergraduate programmes — up from 35 in 2018 — as the Ministry of Education treats degree-portfolio composition as industrial policy. *Strategic implication:* China is systemising AI literacy and portfolio realignment at national speed — a benchmark, and a recruitment-competitiveness signal, for UK institutions deciding how aggressively to rebalance subject mixes. ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/annaesakismith/2026/06/23/china-cuts-12200-university-programs-replaces-many-with-ai-degrees/))

## AI in Teaching & Learning
- **Manchester's four-category assessment policy takes effect (1 September).** Every assessment must be tagged AI-Prohibited, AI-Minimal, AI-Permitted or AI-Integrated — stated on the brief — with group-work, IP and recording rules, and only the University-provided Copilot may touch student-identifiable data. *Strategic implication:* a ready-to-adapt taxonomy that operationalises AI policy into everyday assessment practice — the clearest current model for turning policy from aspiration into the brief itself. ([Manchester](https://documents.manchester.ac.uk/display.aspx?DocID=79331))
- **HEPI: 94% of UK undergraduates use generative AI in assessed work; 12% insert AI text directly.** Reported in a Nature feature on exam redesign, the figures make near-universal AI use an empirical fact rather than a suspicion. *Strategic implication:* the question has moved from "should we permit AI?" to "how do we assess learning when AI is ubiquitous?" — the policy answer is assessment redesign, not detection. ([Nature](https://go.nature.com/3UmuNMu))
- **DEC Global Survey 2026: only 28% of students think assessments reflect AI-era workplace needs.** Across 45,398 respondents in 35 countries, 60% of students worry peers misuse AI for unfair advantage, and US/Canada faculty intent to use AI dropped 9 points to 67%. *Strategic implication:* a measurable assessment–workplace misalignment that erodes the perceived value of degrees — audit assessment portfolios against future-work skills, and expect regional divergence in faculty appetite to affect rollout speed. ([Digital Education Council](https://www.digitaleducationcouncil.com/resource-library-items/ai-in-higher-education-global-survey-2026))
- **King's College London VC: universities must "rethink the essence of learning."** Shitij Kapur argues for shifting from knowledge-delivery to experiential learning — judgement, collaboration and problem-solving — and treating AI as a workplace tool to be learned, not an integrity threat; King's law school already embeds professional-grade AI tools. *Strategic implication:* a leadership-level reframe from policing AI to redefining what assessment is for — a signal for academic boards that the centre of gravity is pedagogy, not integrity policy. ([Korea Times](https://koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/education/k-universities/campus/20260902/kings-calls-on-universities-to-rethink-essence-of-learning-in-ai-era))

## AI Workforce & Skills
- **Government: 37% of AI expert roles require a PhD — skills are concentrated in a narrow workforce.** DSIT's "AI Skills for Life and Work" report finds virtually all AI expert roles require at least a Bachelor's, creating a regional and SME divide in AI access. *Strategic implication:* the credential ceiling is a direct lever for universities as the main AI-talent supplier — but also a warning that the UK skills base is too narrow, so institutions should expand shorter, alternative pathways alongside traditional degrees. ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-skills-for-life-and-work-summary-report/ai-skills-for-life-and-work-summary-report--2))
- **Imperial rolls out stackable "AI for the Workplace" training for all staff via Lifelong Learning.** The autumn-term offer — taught by academics and industry, with tailored modules and manager support — pairs the new AI Strategy Committee with a named in-house upskilling vehicle. *Strategic implication:* a concrete "upskill your own workforce" model that treats staff AI capability as an institutional asset, distinct from student provision and replicable via existing lifelong-learning arms. ([Imperial](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/about/leadership-and-strategy/president/writing-and-speeches/messages-to-the-imperial-community-/growing-ai-ambition/))
- **US: AI competency becomes a graduation requirement — Purdue and Ohio State mandate AI fluency for all students.** At ASU+GSV, leaders stressed AI-resilient skills and public-private partnerships; separately, 35% of US hiring managers plan to hire fewer new graduates in 2026. *Strategic implication:* US institutions are encoding "AI-competent" into the degree credential itself — a signal for UK institutions weighing whether to make AI literacy a formal graduate attribute rather than an elective. ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/18/ai-workforce-college-jobs.html))
- **Huddersfield launches "AI-Ready Yorkshire" to connect SMEs with university AI expertise.** The regional programme positions the university as a local upskilling and adoption partner for small businesses. *Strategic implication:* a template for civic/regional AI missions — deploying institutional AI capability into the local economy, generating CPD and employer-partnership income while closing the SME skills gap the national reports flag. ([Huddersfield](https://www.hud.ac.uk/news/2026/september/ai-ready-yorkshire-connect-business-uni-expertise/))

## AI Policy & Operations
- **A statutory ICO Code of Practice on AI and automated decision-making is coming.** Regulations 2026 No. 425 (laid by DSIT) direct the ICO to produce a code of good practice on personal data in AI/ADM, fulfilling a DUAA commitment. *Strategic implication:* a formal, binding compliance benchmark is on the horizon — institutions should feed into the consultation and align their AI/ADM governance (DPIA, human oversight, contestability) with what the code will codify, ahead of enforcement. ([Legislation.gov.uk](http://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/425/pdfs/uksiem_20260425_en_001.pdf))
- **EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duties are enforceable now — universities must label AI chatbots and AI-generated content.** Anyone interacting with an AI system must be told it is AI, AI-generated media must not be presented as authentic, and deployments must be documented; high-risk education rules remain deferred to December 2027. *Strategic implication:* transparency applies *today* to any UK institution with EU-facing services — label student-facing chatbots and disclose AI content now, and use the 2027 runway to build risk-management and human-oversight documentation for admissions, grading and proctoring. ([KI-Campus](https://ki-campus.eu/en/ki-governance-en/eu-ai-act-universities/))
- **Agentic AI moves university operations from chatbots to governed, workflow-executing agents.** The "agentic AI university" framing describes a student-lifecycle stack — recruitment concierges, Socratic tutors, predictive intervention, credential verification, back-office agents — targeting workflow execution rather than information retrieval. *Strategic implication:* the efficiency case is shifting from productivity tool to automated workflow, carrying governance and workforce-redesign obligations — run agent-readiness audits (data silos, process bottlenecks) and require human approval before agents touch consequential decisions. ([UPCEA](https://upcea.edu/the-rise-of-the-agentic-ai-university-in-2026))
- **UK universities sign a joint statement on DSIT's AI for Science Strategy, positioning HE as the delivery partner.** The statement commits institutions to AI-methods training, conversion courses, AI-for-science fellowships and research-technical-professional support. *Strategic implication:* government is anchoring national AI-for-science delivery in universities — map your offer to the strategy's asks to secure funding and policy influence as delivery begins. ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-for-science-strategy-uk-universities-joint-statement/ai-for-science-strategy-uk-universities-joint-statement))

## What this means for your institution
1. **Treat vendor consolidation as the strategic decision, not tool adoption.** With Manchester, Leicester and Roehampton locking into Copilot and Gemini, procurement should negotiate switching costs, data-residency and exit terms before peers lock in — and recognise a genuine two-vendor market now exists.
2. **Operationalise assessment policy with a categorisation framework.** Manchester's Prohibited/Minimal/Permitted/Integrated model, stated on every brief, turns AI policy into daily practice — adopt or adapt it before the term is fully underway.
3. **Upskill staff as the binding constraint.** Pearson, DEC and Imperial's new Lifelong Learning offer all point the same way: faculty and staff capability — not tool access — determines graduate AI-readiness.
4. **Act now on the compliance already enforceable.** EU Article 50 transparency applies today to EU-facing services; feed into the ICO's AI/ADM code consultation and audit admissions, grading and proctoring ahead of the December 2027 high-risk deadline.

## Worth watching
- **Roehampton's Gemini rollout** as the first real test of a two-vendor market — whether Google gains traction against Copilot, and what data-residency and exit terms its managed deployments carry.
- **The ICO's Code of Practice consultation** on AI and automated decision-making — the most actionable institutional submission this quarter for governance teams.
- **The 2 December 2026 watermarking/disclosure grace period** under the EU AI Act — a nearer compliance milestone than the 2027 high-risk date. ([CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-deadline-delay))
- **Whether UK institutions follow the US lead** in making AI competency a formal graduation requirement or graduate attribute.
