# Fluency Without Competence — Week of September 14, 2026

## Executive brief
This week the sector got the number it has been avoiding: AI adoption in UK architectural practice jumped from 59% to 74% in a single year — while only 17% of practices say their designs are actually better for it, 61% say AI will make it harder for early-career professionals to acquire essential skills, and 59% expect staff reductions (RIBA AI Report 2026). The same pattern runs through education. Students raise homework scores by ~18% with AI while unaided exam performance falls; a controlled study finds AI detectors flag *guideline-compliant* editing at 38–80% while deliberate evasion goes essentially undetected; and the University of Southampton has walked away from Turnitin over student-work training clauses. Policy infrastructure is lagging adoption rather than leading it — 41% of UK universities still have no findable AI policy, only 19 of 26 leading studios have a written AI policy, and Missouri's president announced a universal AI course requirement before faculty governance had approved it. The through-line for leadership: the constraint is no longer access or licence, it is **evidence** — of learning, of competence, of authorship, of how an AI output was checked. This week's highest-ROI moves are assessment redesign that produces independent evidence, an AI overlay for studio and practice-based assessment, contracting that closes data-training loopholes, and an agent policy written before students and staff bring their own.

## AI Strategy & Governance
- **The architect of the UK's AI strategy resigns as ARIA chair over his Anthropic role — a documented conflict-of-interest test.** Matt Clifford stepped down on 7 September after the Commons Science, Innovation and Technology Committee chair Chi Onwurah called his plan to stay on as founding chair while managing international affairs at Anthropic a "clear conflict of interest." He remains until 6 November with safeguards in place while a replacement is found; Onwurah says questions remain over how the situation arose and what assessments were undertaken. *Strategic implication:* expect the same scrutiny of your own declarations — governors, advisory boards and national AI bodies now need published conflict-of-interest positions, not private assurances. ([THE](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/matt-clifford-resigns-aria-chair-over-anthropic-role))
- **Two-in-five UK universities still have no AI policy a student, parent or regulator can find — and where a policy sits determines what it does.** HEPI Policy Note 71 scraped all 163 degree-awarding institutions (16 February): only 96 had discoverable policies; 86% of those used the vocabulary of learning while operating as compliance instruments, and close reading showed a policy's *location* is what shapes its effect — one hosted in a misconduct framework "inherits that framework's assumptions about students, whatever vocabulary softens the message." Durham, Stirling, Canterbury Christ Church and Arts University Plymouth remain the exemplars. *Strategic implication:* policy discoverability and policy location are now the measurable governance gaps — audit both before you write another word of policy. ([HEPI](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/what-uk-university-ai-policies-actually-do-a-study-of-96-institutions/)) ([THE](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/university-ai-policies-promise-support-deliver-surveillance))
- **US: a universal AI course requirement was announced before faculty governance approved it.** At the University of Missouri, President Mun Choi publicly described an AI requirement for all undergraduates while the proposal was still in development and had not cleared curriculum approval; USC separately found it cannot measure the environmental footprint of institution-wide AI because vendors publish no emissions or water data. *Strategic implication:* the sequence of an AI decision can undo a strategic priority as easily as its content — take AI requirements through Senate and curriculum committees first, and make vendor transparency a procurement condition. ([AHigherVision brief](https://linkedin.com/pulse/ahighervisions-ai-higher-education-daily-intelligence-fridrich-as0xe))
- **★ Featured · China — AI becomes a basic public course in every university, with AI literacy written into teacher certification.** The five-ministry "AI + Education" Action Plan requires AI in the core curriculum for all students, subject-specific textbooks, interdisciplinary "X + AI" programmes, and a national teacher AI-literacy standard with AI added to teacher-qualification exams. Fudan runs 100+ AI courses and 41 "X + AI" dual degrees; Beijing mandates at least eight hours of AI per student per year; the 2030 goal is a literacy system "vertically integrated across all stages of education and horizontally connected across society." *Strategic implication:* the strongest comparator for UK institutions weighing whether AI literacy becomes a formal graduate attribute — and for schools deciding whether studio AI capability is assessed or merely permitted. ([GOV.CN](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202604/15/content_WS69df29e6c6d00ca5f9a0a6b1.html))

## AI in Teaching & Learning
- **"Reviewing AI-generated work is not a basic checking exercise — it requires the judgement that entry-level tasks used to build."** Wonkhe's case from the University of Surrey makes the term's sharpest pedagogical argument: supervise AI before learning the standards of the field and the result is "fluency without competence." Surrey is embedding discipline-specific AI teaching into **every degree programme — foundation, undergraduate and postgraduate — from September 2026**, explicitly refusing to treat AI as a generic digital skill, a misconduct risk or a bolt-on employability module. *Strategic implication:* decide, discipline by discipline, where AI builds judgement and where it substitutes for the experience judgement is made of — for studio subjects that decision belongs in project briefs, portfolio criteria and review protocols. ([Wonkhe](https://wonkhe.com/blogs/ai-literacy-is-not-enough-universities-must-teach-through-disciplinary-standards/))
- **AI-assisted homework scores are rising while unaided exam performance falls — the output/learning gap is now measurable.** Reporting on controlled studies (2 September) puts homework scores ~18% higher and time per assignment ~30% lower, with weaker later exam performance; Tufts faculty report the same pattern and conclude their governance structure was solving the wrong problem — the gap is not policy inconsistency but assignments that no longer prove what a student knows. MIT Sloan's two-year field study finds experimentation stalls where testing, refining and support are left to individuals without structure. *Strategic implication:* treat this as a learning-assurance issue before an integrity issue — audit which tasks still produce independent evidence, and rebuild those that no longer do. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/2/faster-homework-poor-exam-results-what-ai-is-doing-to-students-learning)) ([AHigherVision](https://linkedin.com/pulse/ahighervisions-ai-higher-education-daily-intelligence-fridrich-hgp7c))
- **Detection has failed as integrity infrastructure — and a Russell Group university has left the market leader.** A controlled study of published abstracts (arXiv 2608.11256) finds guideline-compliant "refine only" AI edits flagged at 38–80%, unmodified human originals at 9–15% (non-STEM far above STEM), and AI-text humanizers evading detection almost entirely (post-humanization detection <4%). The authors conclude detector scores "should not serve as standalone misconduct evidence." Southampton will not renew its Turnitin contract beyond 2026–27 over proposals that would have allowed student submissions to train AI services; Turnitin denies training on student work but acknowledges "anonymised submissions" may improve its tools. *Strategic implication:* honest editing currently carries higher sanction risk than deliberate evasion — move integrity spend into assessment design and put data-training clauses in every AI/academic-integrity contract. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11256)) ([THE](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/southampton-dumps-turnitin-over-use-students-work-train-ai))
- **Studio culture is where the authorship question is actually being settled.** Dezeen's survey of 53 leading studios (26 responded) found 25 of those actively encourage AI use but **only 19 have a written AI policy**; more than half have built in-house tools (Foster + Partners, Gensler, OMA, MVRDV, Hassell, Heatherwick); Claude is the most-used model at 44%; CF Møller reports Photoshop usage down 80%. Denmark's Utzon Center opened *I'm not a robot: Architecture and Design Between Human and Machine*, featuring a home concept by humanoid robot artist Ai-Da, while the University at Buffalo's Next-Gen Architect graduate studio concluded that authorship, not efficiency, is the unresolved question — "it raises more questions than it answers." Controlled studio evidence is emerging: the *Gen-AI-tecture* study reports a local ComfyUI workflow improving design form and aesthetics by ~14%, with students worried about their visual skills reporting no disadvantage. *Strategic implication:* write the authorship standard into studio assessment — and model professional practice by holding a written AI policy of your own. ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2026/08/25/architecture-studios-encouraging-ai-feature/)) ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/?p=2288052)) ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.21361))

## AI Workforce & Skills
- **RIBA AI Report 2026: adoption has tipped over, and the profession is openly worried about early careers.** The third annual survey records the jump from 59% to 74% of practices using AI on most projects, alongside 59% expecting staff reductions, 61% saying AI will make it harder for early-career professionals to gain essential skills and experience, and only 17% agreeing their designs are better because of AI. RIBA will publish an **AI Overlay to the RIBA Plan of Work** and a responsible-use policy this year, tied to its "Repeal, Reserve, Regulate" competence campaign. *Strategic implication:* the tasks that build junior competence are the tasks being automated — for a school of architecture this is a curriculum and placement question: design the replacement for the learning that used to happen in the drawing office. ([RIBA](https://riba.org/news/ai-adoption-reaches-tipping-point-across-uk-architecture)) ([Beale & Co](https://beale-law.com/article/riba-ai-report-2026-charts-the-growing-role-of-artificial-intelligence-in-architectural-practice/))
- **The productivity evidence is concrete — and concentrated in image production, documentation and review.** Zaha Hadid Architects reports renderings in 20% of the time previously required and up to 50% productivity gains in mid-stage design preparation, having moved from Midjourney/Gendo to proprietary systems; NVIDIA's account of ZHA's Omniverse/OpenUSD pipeline records 3x more iterative cycles and client feedback loops falling from a week to under 24 hours. Foster + Partners' ARD group has built *Ask Foster + Partners* across six decades of project knowledge and warns about **"review debt"** — generative models producing hundreds of variations that "mask embedded errors, inconsistencies, or biases." *Strategic implication:* verification and judgement are the new graduate capability; teach specification literacy, checking and traceability, not prompt familiarity. ([Building Design](https://www.bdonline.co.uk/news/patrik-schumacher-claims-ai-keeps-zha-a-step-ahead/5133607.article)) ([Foster + Partners](https://www.fosterandpartners.com/news/the-column-sherif-eltarabishy-on-technology-research-and-development))
- **Agentic AI is now targeting studio operations, not just image-making.** Avoice has launched what it presents as the first AI agents to run the operational side of an architecture studio — specification writing and drawing review, project tasks and schedules, studio finances, invoicing and timesheets — explicitly *not* intervening in design authorship. SOM reports "AI and agent-based workflows" for documentation automation, data cleanup and long-form document review; OMA and Snøhetta describe AI mainly as a way to offload repetitive verification. *Strategic implication:* this is the "coding agents in AEC" thesis arriving in practice — professional education should weight systems integration, QA and regulatory reasoning. ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2026/07/24/avoice-ai-agents-architecture-studios/))
- **Graduate demand is narrowing even as AI-specific entry routes open.** UK employers are opening dedicated data-and-AI graduate programmes for 2026–27 (Lloyds Banking Group at £48,500 plus benefits; EY Technology & AI Engineering; Santander Data & AI), while job adverts listing AI literacy rose 61% in 2024 and PwC puts the AI-skills wage premium at 56%. DSIT's AI Skills Boost has passed **1,001,147 completed courses** and added the Chartered Management Institute as a strategic partner behind the 10-million-workers-by-2030 ambition (2m in SMEs), with £27m for TechLocal and an AI Skills Boost Hub procurement under way. Only about **26% of architecture degree holders work as architects.** *Strategic implication:* partner with the national upskilling programme rather than duplicating it, and treat portfolio evidence of AI-augmented delivery as the employability asset. ([Wonkhe](https://wonkhe.com/blogs/ai-literacy-is-not-enough-universities-must-teach-through-disciplinary-standards/)) ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-skills-boost-explainer/ai-skills-boost-explainer)) ([CMI](https://www.managers.org.uk/about-cmi/media-centre/press-releases/cmi-selected-to-partner-with-government-to-support-ai-skills-boost/))

## AI Policy & Operations
- **The EU AI Act's education timetable is now two dates — and the nearer one is a transparency duty.** Article 4's AI-literacy obligation, live since February 2025, was rewoven by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (the Digital Omnibus), in force since 27 July 2026: it now requires providers and deployers to *support the development of* AI literacy among staff and others operating their systems, with no mandated level. Annex III high-risk education uses — admissions, assessment, proctoring — were deferred to **2 December 2027**. Article 50 transparency applies from 2 August 2026, and synthetic-content systems placed on the market before then must comply by **2 December 2026**. *Strategic implication:* "the AI Act slipped to 2027" is an oversimplification — document AI-literacy provision and label AI content and chatbots, hitting the nearer December milestone. ([Substack](https://francescomasera.substack.com/p/europe-made-ai-literacy-a-legal-duty)) ([Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/technology/1841290/ai-act-implementation))
- **The ICO opens consultation on agentic AI guidance this month.** The regulator's published plan lists Agentic AI guidance at drafting stage with public consultation **due to launch September 2026** and a final version in **Spring 2027**, alongside an automated-decision-making and profiling update this winter, anonymisation/pseudonymisation guidance for research, and guidance on AI-generated subject access requests. The ICO's AI and data-protection guidance remains under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act. *Strategic implication:* agent permissions, identity and liability are the next compliance surface in student services and research support — contribute to the consultation and document agent scope now. ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/what-we-do/our-plans-for-new-and-updated-guidance/technology))
- **ARB consults on a modernised Prescribed Examination — closing 16 September 2026.** The proposals replace the Part 1/Part 2-era examination with an Application → Review → online Interview route assessed against ARB's new **Academic Competency Outcomes**, open to applicants holding a Level 7 (RQF) qualification in architecture or a relevant discipline, with the revised exam expected in spring 2027. *Strategic implication:* this is the registration architecture that assessment, portfolios and studio outcomes must map to — the final week to influence how "equivalent academic competence" is judged. ([ARB](https://arb.org.uk/arb-consults-on-proposals-for-modernised-prescribed-examination/))
- **Agentic AI raises identity, equity and assurance questions — and Jisc's line is that silence creates risk.** Jisc's National Centre for AI frames the governance decision as *where* human approval must always be required: low-risk drafting and retrieval versus higher-stakes actions that access sensitive data, change records or trigger formal decisions. Granting agents full user access raises identity, liability and academic-integrity issues, so tightly scoped service accounts and agent-specific permission models are proposed. The student-facing version is **BYOA — bringing your own agent** — where a device merely provides access but an agent can *initiate actions*, with equity risks where access to capable agents is uneven. Jisc/EAUC's AI sustainability guide and THE's reporting show institutions cannot yet measure their own AI emissions or water use. *Strategic implication:* publish an agent policy this term — permitted actions, approval points, data boundaries and BYOA expectations — and ask vendors for energy and water data in every AI procurement. ([Jisc](https://nationalcentreforai.jiscinvolve.org/wp/2026/02/12/agentic-ai-roundtable-governance-autonomy-and-the-future-of-educational-agents)) ([Jisc/EAUC](https://repository.jisc.ac.uk/10589/1/AI-sustainability-guide-EAUC-partnership.pdf)) ([THE](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/depth/universities-ai-embrace-undermining-their-net-zero-targets))

## What this means for your institution
1. **Write it down and make it findable.** 41% of UK universities have no discoverable AI policy and only 19 of 26 leading studios have one. Publish a current policy in a learning-and-teaching framework (not a misconduct one), and hold an equivalent written AI policy for professional practice in studio and live projects.
2. **Rebuild assessment so it produces independent evidence.** The homework/exam divergence and QAA's within-institution variability point the same way: define which tasks evidence unaided competence, and add accountability moments — developmental vivas, oral defence of portfolio work, in-review explanation of AI-assisted output.
3. **Stop funding detection as integrity infrastructure, and close the data loopholes.** With compliant editing flagged at up to 80% and humanized text evading detection, redirect that spend to assessment design and add explicit no-training-on-student-work clauses to AI and integrity vendor contracts.
4. **Write the agent and authorship rules before the year hardens.** Publish an agent policy (permitted actions, human approval points, data boundaries, BYOA expectations for students) and an AI overlay for studio/professional assessment that maps where AI is legitimate and where authorship and accountability stay human — aligned to the ARB Academic Competency Outcomes and the coming RIBA Plan of Work overlay.
