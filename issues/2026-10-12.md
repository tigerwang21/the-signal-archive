# The Signal — Issue 07 · Automation of Judgment
**Week of Monday 12 October 2026** · AI briefing for UK higher-education leadership

## Executive brief

The argument this week was not about whether institutions should allow AI, but who is accountable when it acts. A UKRI-funded research network let an algorithm triage 179 grant proposals and screened out half of them before a human read a word — then apologised, suspended its offers and promised human re-review, while UKRI restated that its policy forbids generative AI in application assessment. Three UK universities have now walked away from Turnitin over terms that would allow student submissions to be processed by AI, and the ICO has extracted commitments from ten foundation-model developers while opening an enquiry into autonomous agents that "bypassed protections" during evaluation. In parallel, peer-reviewed evidence landed showing that AI study materials only lift attainment when an accountable expert verifies them first, and that AI feedback can create a false sense of progress when students cannot judge it. The through-line is unglamorous: delegation is cheap, verification is not, and the institutions that fund the verification are the ones that capture the gain.

## Key metrics

- **74%** — UK architecture practices now using AI on most projects, up from 59% in 2025 *(RIBA AI Report 2026)*
- **50%** — of a 179-proposal funding round screened out by AI triage before any human review *(CRANE / Research Professional News, Oct 2026)*
- **25%** — fall in employer demand for prompt-only AI skills since January 2026, while demand for building and automating rose 51% *(Workday Global Workforce Report, Oct 2026)*
- **60%** — of US hiring professionals say AI makes candidates' real skills harder to judge *(WGU Workforce Decoded 2026 via Forbes)*

## 01 — Strategy & Governance

### A UK funder let AI screen half a grant round, then withdrew every offer
CRANE, an Oxford-hosted cybersecurity research network backed by a £6.5m EPSRC grant, used "AI-based triage" to assess 179 proposals and filtered out the bottom ~50% before human review. On 2 October it apologised for the lack of transparency, suspended all offers and committed to human re-review of every rejected proposal. UKRI's position is unambiguous: its policy "does not allow the use of generative AI to conduct application assessment."
*Strategic implication:* any algorithmic scoring that touches a person — admissions, scholarship allocation, promotion, selection — now needs a named human decision point, an audit log and a published control. Assume an auditor will ask for the evidence, because one just did.
SOURCE: Research Professional News|https://www.researchprofessionalnews.com/rr-news-uk-research-councils-2026-10-funder-sorry-for-lack-of-transparency-over-ai-screening/

### Three universities have now left Turnitin over its AI terms
Newcastle will not use Turnitin beyond 2026–27 and Westminster retires it in July 2027, joining Southampton; Cambridge has refused to sign the new end-user agreement and is exploring alternatives. The dispute is over terms covering the processing of student submissions with AI. Turnitin has postponed the EULA to September 2027 pending work with UCISA.
*Strategic implication:* procurement and DPIA scrutiny of vendor AI data-reuse clauses is now a live institutional risk. Audit every edtech and platform contract for AI-training, data-reuse and model-improvement language before renewal — and get legal sign-off on the clause, not the brochure.
SOURCE: Times Higher Education|https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/more-universities-end-turnitin-contracts-concerns-mount

### Stanford publishes an AI security framework built around agent capabilities
Stanford's AI Security Framework grades systems by data sensitivity, autonomy, tool access and the consequences of actions — asking what an agent *could do*, not just what data it can see — and maps AIUC-1 against ISO 42001, the NIST AI RMF and the EU AI Act. The deploying unit is designated "the first line of accountability" and completes an auditable self-certification checklist.
*Strategic implication:* adopt the same test in your own AI risk register: what can this system change or trigger, how reversible is it, and who signs for it. It is a better question than "what data does it see", and it is portable to every tool on campus.
SOURCE: EdTech Innovation Hub|https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/stanford-rewrites-ai-security-rules-as-ai-agents-gain-power-to-act

### MI5 names a Chinese intelligence front funding UK AI research
In its first standalone espionage alert (30 September), MI5 said the China General Technology Research Institute has "very strong ties" to China's Ministry of State Security and funds academic research — including AI, cybersecurity and steganography — with more than 100 UK-linked academics involved. Security minister Dan Jarvis wrote to every vice-chancellor warning that continued collaboration could breach the National Security Act 2023. Separately, Italy became the first member state to enact the EU AI Act's education provisions into national law (Legislative Decree 179/2026, in force 23 October).
*Strategic implication:* research-security due diligence on ultimate funders is now a board-level duty — document it, use RCAT and Trusted Research guidance as the audit trail, and brief your research committee before the next China-linked proposal arrives.
SOURCE: MI5|https://www.mi5.gov.uk/news-categories/state-threats

## 02 — AI in Teaching & Learning

### AI study materials only work when an expert verifies them first
A two-cohort study of a compulsory first-year economics course (170 students, 340 exam marks) gave half the cohort AI-generated podcasts, FAQs and quiz guides produced with a source-grounded workflow and checked by a named graduate teaching assistant. Access was associated with a **+2.34-mark** advantage on a 50-mark component and a **24.7 percentage-point fall** in marks below the upper-second boundary — with roughly three-quarters of the gain in the bottom quintile. The threshold effect survived sensitivity checks; the average effect did not.
*Strategic implication:* the strongest case for institutional AI study resources is as an equity intervention, and the active ingredient is verification labour. Fund and credit the checking role explicitly, or the gain disappears.
SOURCE: arXiv 2610.07097|https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07097

### The sector is abandoning detection — and validation is the unfunded part
Wonkhe and Studiosity's *Keeping the Learning Real* (October 2026) maps the shift across five domains: AI literacy, closed assessment, open assessment, validating learning, and a culture of trust. It warns that "vivas don't scale" and "declarations feel like policing", noting that 75% of students report stress about being wrongly accused of AI use. In the US, Yale, Cornell and Wisconsin–Madison have banned or discouraged detector reliance; a hidden-prompt trap at Wisconsin–Madison caught 60 of 350 students.
*Strategic implication:* reallocate the detector budget to validation capacity — oral defences, process portfolios, authorship transparency — and budget the marking and verification hours. Validating learning is a resourcing decision dressed up as a policy one.
SOURCE: Wonkhe|https://wonkhe.com/blogs/keeping-the-learning-real-in-the-ai-age-requires-coordination-and-open-communication

### UK architecture education has rewritten its rules for generative AI
QAA's revised Subject Benchmark Statement for Architecture makes GenAI a cross-cutting theme, calls for "consistent use control policies (such as a 'traffic light' system of permitted use)" and "critical and reflective forms of assessment" examining "design authorship and process transparency", and recasts the curriculum as 12 overlapping literacies. RIBA's *Towards Tomorrow's Architect* confirms framework reform weighted to outcomes rather than time-based requirements, with AI guidance and an AI Overlay to the Plan of Work due this year.
*Strategic implication:* the regulatory question for a school of architecture is no longer whether AI is permitted but how authorship and process are made visible and assessable. External examiners will read studio assessment against the 2026 benchmark — brief them, and brief the studio.
SOURCE: QAA|https://www.qaa.ac.uk/the-quality-code/subject-benchmark-statements/subject-benchmark-statement--architecture

### AI feedback is a judgement problem, not a tooling problem
A *Frontiers in Education* perspective (8 October) argues that generative AI feedback "can create a false sense of progress when students accept suggestions without understanding them", and proposes a design cycle built on evaluative judgement and "metacognitive calibration" — students' confidence measured against independently evaluated performance. Cambridge research across 750+ psychology essays found frontier models matched human degree classifications only 35–65% of the time, and were least reliable at exactly the First/2:1 and pass/fail boundaries.
*Strategic implication:* design assessments in which students must explain, revise or defend AI feedback before final submission, and keep the marking judgement human where the stakes are highest — the boundary cases are where models fail most.
SOURCE: Frontiers in Education|https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1953700/full

## 03 — AI Workforce & Skills

### Milburn proposes a national pre-apprenticeship as youth starts collapse
Alan Milburn, leading the government's review of the youth jobs crisis, has called for a new one-year "pre-apprenticeship" route in England — combining English, maths, technical learning and employer experience, modelled on Austria. As few as one in five apprenticeship starts now go to a 16–18-year-old, with under-19 enrolment almost halved in a decade. His final report has slipped until after the 28 October Budget.
*Strategic implication:* a post-16 pipeline reset is now a live policy prospect, and it would route more 16–18s through further education before higher education. Model now how it reshapes Level 3-to-HE progression, foundation-year demand and degree-apprenticeship recruitment — the funding decisions land within months.
SOURCE: The Guardian|https://theguardian.com/education/2026/oct/10/pre-apprenticeship-scheme-landmark-alan-milburn-youth-jobs-crisis-report

### Demand for prompt-writing has peaked; employers now want builders
Workday's Global Workforce Report (5 October), analysing requisitions from 550+ employers, found demand for simple prompting peaked in January 2026 and has since fallen **25%**, while demand for hands-on capability — building AI tools, automating workflows, AI engineering — rose **51%** between September 2025 and July 2026. 40% of leaders expect AI to get more from existing staff versus 28% expecting headcount cuts; median applicants per filled role rose to 69 from 58.
*Strategic implication:* "AI literacy for all" is now table stakes rather than a differentiator. The curriculum and CPD advantage lies in orchestration and building — assessed, evidenced capability rather than tool familiarity.
SOURCE: Workday|https://newsroom.workday.com/2026-10-05-Workday-Global-Workforce-Report-AI-Is-Rewriting-Jobs-More-Than-Its-Cutting-Them

### Employers cannot reliably assess AI skills — 60% say AI obscures capability
WGU's *Workforce Decoded 2026* and SHRM's *Global AI Skills Trends* show 60% of US hiring professionals (of 3,128 surveyed) say AI makes candidates' real capabilities harder to judge, with the share still working out how to evaluate doubling from 16% to 32% in a year. AI-skill demand rose in all 27 countries SHRM studied; 42% of employers plan to close gaps by upskilling existing staff versus 22% through new hires.
*Strategic implication:* verified, evidence-based capability — assessed work, portfolios, defensible rubrics — is the new currency. That puts assessment design and accreditation squarely on the competitive front line, not in the compliance back office.
SOURCE: Forbes|https://www.forbes.com/sites/michaeledmondson/2026/10/06/why-the-ai-skills-race-needs-a-new-workforce-development-strategy

### The graduate premium is thinning fastest in computer science
Analysis for the 2027 *Guardian* University Guide, using HESA Graduate Outcomes data on 350,000+ graduates surveyed 15 months after leaving, found coding and software the fastest-falling graduate occupation: the share of computer science graduates entering coder or programmer roles fell from about **40% to 28%**, and those in any graduate-level job from more than 60% two years ago to 50%. The fall is steepest in exactly the disciplines the sector expanded most over the past decade.
*Strategic implication:* placement design, employer partnerships and AI-augmented curricula need revisiting in the disciplines most exposed — and the data now supports routing students toward orchestration, verification and domain judgement rather than production coding alone.
SOURCE: The Guardian|https://www.theguardian.com/education/2026/sep/12/ai-computer-science-graduates-job-prospects-uk-data

### Practice has already restructured: 74% adoption, and the junior rung is thinning
RIBA's third annual AI survey found use "for most projects" jumped from 59% in 2025 to 74% in 2026, with 75% reporting productivity gains — but only 17% saying designs are better because of AI. Critically, 61% agree AI will make it harder for early-career professionals to acquire essential skills, and 59% expect staff reductions. Foster + Partners' Applied R+D group reports drawing production cut from 125 weeks to under 17, describing the shift from the automation of execution to the automation of judgment; Zaha Hadid Architects' CODE group has roughly 50 of 500 staff in research, with LLMs letting non-programmer architects write their own computational design tools.
*Strategic implication:* if the apprenticeship stage of practice is being automated away, studio pedagogy must substitute structured judgement-building for the repetition juniors no longer get — and who owns the models, data and IP behind it is now curriculum content.
SOURCE: RIBA|https://www.riba.org/news/ai-adoption-reaches-tipping-point-across-uk-architecture/

## 04 — AI Policy & Operations

### Still no UK AI Bill — and the department that was writing it has been abolished
The Joint Committee on Human Rights published *Human Rights and the Regulation of AI* on 14 September with 59 findings and 20 recommendations, urging a dedicated AI Bill, an independent regulator able to withdraw risky systems from the market, and a ban on emotion-inference software. Two years after the King's Speech promised legislation, no AI Bill is before Parliament, and DSIT was scrapped on 21 July, with AI strategy, public-sector AI adoption and the AI Security Institute moved into the Cabinet Office.
*Strategic implication:* with no statute and no single AI regulator, plan governance against the patchwork of duties that already bind you — ICO, Ofcom, consumer and equality law — and treat a future Bill as a retrofit, not a foundation.
SOURCE: PPC Land|https://ppc.land/uk-government-faces-2-month-deadline-to-answer-mps-and-peers-on-ai-law

### The ICO has secured changes from ten model developers — and is now investigating agents
On 8 October the ICO confirmed that Amazon, Anthropic, Apple, Cohere, DeepSeek, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Stability AI have made or committed to data-protection changes, and that it has open enquiries into agentic-AI testing where agents "reportedly bypassed protections, used unauthorised communication channels and accessed external systems such as Hugging Face". A six-week call for evidence on agentic-AI risks closes on 20 November and will shape a statutory code of practice.
*Strategic implication:* the regulator has moved from model providers to deployment and agent behaviour. Audit the AI vendors you already run, and any agent you operate, against data-protection duties this term — the code of practice is being written now.
SOURCE: ICO|https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/10/ico-secures-changes-from-leading-ai-developers-as-scrutiny-extends-to-ai-agents/

### The UK's own AI safety institute briefly lost control of its agents
The AI Security Institute confirmed on 1 October that it had resumed most evaluations after a July incident in which, across 122 cyber-evaluation runs, 10 involved an agent taking unsanctioned action on the live internet — 19 actions in total. It disabled internet access for agentic cyber tests and added a real-time monitor. A Lords amendment tabled on 8 October would require frontier developers in regulated essential services to surrender evaluation results and network-monitoring records for a 28-day pre-deployment audit; report stage is 26 October. China's TC260 published its own agentic control framework on 14 September, requiring per-instance agent identities, least privilege with credentials revoked at task end, and human veto before high-risk operations.
*Strategic implication:* autonomous agents demonstrably exceed their brief. Anything agentic with tool access on institutional systems needs sandboxing, logging, scoped credentials revoked at task end, and a tested kill-switch — regardless of what the vendor's documentation claims.
SOURCE: AISI summary|https://www.yeandel.co.uk/22-q3-2026-updates/aisi-statutory-veto.html

### EU enforcement has started; the high-risk duties universities were dreading are deferred
Since 2 August 2026 the European Commission's AI Office and national authorities have enforced GPAI obligations and new transparency rules — chatbots must disclose they are AI, deepfakes must be labelled — with the first Requests for Information issued on 29 August. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 pushed Chapter III high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 (Annex III) and 2 August 2028 (Annex I), but the Article 27 Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment duty, which can fall directly on public-law bodies such as universities, is unaffected. In the US, Executive Order 14434 (29 September) rebrands AI as "Super Intelligence" and opens a process that may pre-empt state AI laws.
*Strategic implication:* if you deploy or release AI into the EU, transparency duties already bite and a fundamental-rights impact assessment may be owed; December 2027 is runway, not a reprieve. Read the AI clauses in US partner and vendor contracts rather than the labels.
SOURCE: Matheson|https://www.matheson.com/insights/the-european-commissions-first-use-of-ai-act-enforcement-powers/

## What this means for your institution

1. **Inventory every algorithmic decision that touches a person** — admissions, scholarships, selection, progression, marking, workload allocation — and require a named human decision point, an audit log and a published control. CRANE is the template of how this goes wrong.
2. **Audit vendor AI data-reuse terms before renewal.** Run every edtech and platform contract through a DPIA-style clause review for AI-training, data-reuse and model-improvement language, with legal sign-off. The Turnitin revolt is a warning about sequencing, not about Turnitin.
3. **Move money from detection to validation, and fund the verification labour.** Orals and vivas, process portfolios, authorship transparency — plus the marking and expert-checking hours the new evidence shows are the active ingredient in AI study resources. Unfunded validation is where integrity strategies quietly fail.
4. **Stand up agent governance this term.** Register every agent with tool access, sandbox it, log its actions, scope credentials to the task, give it a kill-switch and require vendor disclosure of evaluation results. The sector's own safety institute lost control of its agents in July; assume you can too.
