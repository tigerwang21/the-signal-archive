# The Signal — Issue 10 · The Meter and the Clause
**Week of Monday 2 November 2026** · AI briefing for UK higher-education leadership

## Executive brief

Cognizant announced on Friday that it has put more than 25,000 professionals behind the delivery of OpenAI products, organised into a Frontier Capability Group that begins with AI-native software engineering, application modernisation and agentic customer operations. The same week, Oracle opened an OpenAI services practice at AI World, ServiceNow placed agent actions behind its own permissions and business rules, and Anthropic put pooled budgets and spend reporting inside the enterprise console. The frontier laboratories have spent four years selling access. This week they sold delivery, and the delivery is staffed by other firms' people. On the design side the same shift arrived as a price list: Autodesk began charging for its AEC Data Model API on 6 October at one Flex token for every 5 MB exported beyond the allowance, which prices a design workflow by the movement of data rather than by the person sitting at the screen.

The clause arrived in the same seven days. On Monday 26 October the Lords carried, by 202 to 155, an amendment requiring a review of whether the AI Security Institute should be able to audit frontier systems before deployment. Government Amendment 70 went through without a division and allows regulations to hand the Secretary of State powers where a network compromise "is or may be attributable to loss of oversight or control of an AI agent", including the appointment of inspectors, search of premises, demands for information held outside the United Kingdom and financial penalties. The minister's argument against giving the institute a regulatory role was that regulation would cost the access that voluntary partnership buys. On Tuesday, Jisc published a sector statement signed by Universities UK, GuildHE and the Association of Colleges that asks suppliers for six things, amongst them visibility of usage and charging, value that holds over the life of a contract, continuity of a named model version and no lock-in.

For a chief technology officer in a smaller or specialist institution, those two documents describe the shape of the next three years. Consumption will be metered in units your finance office has never modelled, and the duties that follow an agent's mistakes will land on the operator of the system. The instruments available are unglamorous: a usage ledger you can query without asking a supplier, a schedule of requirements attached to every contract before signature, and a register of which agent holds which delegated permission. Institutions that build those three things this year will be able to answer the question that arrives first from a council, an insurer or a regulator: what did the system do, on whose authority, at what cost.

The other three beats describe the same gap in different rooms. Employers expect AI to remake graduate and apprentice roles, 87 per cent of them by the reckoning of the Institute of Student Employers, and 43 per cent say those roles have already changed without being formally redesigned. Cambridge's own Board of Scrutiny found the university's central guidance on AI to be "sometimes contradictory and vague". Architects expect the profession to shed staff at twice the rate they expect their own practices to, and 61 per cent of them say early-career training is getting harder. Every one of those findings points at assessment and instrumentation. None of them is solved by another policy statement.

## Key metrics

- **25,000** — professionals aligned to delivering OpenAI products in Cognizant's new Frontier Capability Group *(Cognizant, 30 October 2026)*
- **6** — areas of expectation set for AI suppliers in the Jisc sector statement backed by Universities UK, GuildHE and the Association of Colleges *(Jisc, 27 October 2026)*
- **202–155** — Lords majority for a review of whether the AI Security Institute should audit frontier systems before deployment *(Hansard, 26 October 2026)*
- **87%** — employers expecting AI to reshape graduate and apprentice roles, against 43 per cent who say the change has happened without redesign *(ISE Student Development Survey 2026, via HEPI)*

## 01 — Strategy & Governance

### The sector writes its own schedule of requirements and hands it to suppliers
Jisc's statement of 27 October sets six areas of expectation for providers of AI-enabled products: transparency at the point of signature, protection of value over the life of the agreement, visibility of usage metrics and charging mechanisms, continuity of a specific model version where teaching or research depends on it, controls over data and governance, and interoperability that allows a change of supplier. It asks that these become standard elements of procurement and negotiation, and it ships with a due diligence framework that tests individual products against them. Caren Milloy, Jisc's director of licensing, framed the purpose as keeping institutions in control of their costs, choices and risks as adoption scales.
*Strategic implication:* a shared negotiating text changes the position of a small institution. The six areas can be attached to every renewal as the institution's own schedule of requirements, and a supplier that will not answer them in writing has given you a decision you can defend to a board.
SOURCE: Jisc|https://www.jisc.ac.uk/news/all/education-and-research-sector-calls-for-governance-and-cost-control-for-AI

### Peers force a review of pre-deployment audit whilst ministers take powers over agents
At report stage on 26 October, Amendment 163 carried by 202 to 155 and wrote into the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill a review of whether the AI Security Institute should have power to audit frontier systems ahead of deployment, with a deadline of a year after Royal Assent. An amendment placing an incident reporting duty on developers fell 95 to 165. Government Amendment 70 was agreed without a division and permits regulations to give the Secretary of State functions where a compromise of a regulated network is attributable to loss of oversight or control of an AI agent. The minister told peers that a regulatory role "would undermine its voluntary partnership with leading AI developers", and Viscount Colville noted that Anthropic's Claude Mythos 5.1 had been withdrawn from the institute the previous month.
*Strategic implication:* the Bill puts the reporting and containment burden on operators, which includes any institution running agents across estate, finance or admissions. The register of agent permissions and the incident log are becoming the evidence base for a statutory duty, and they cost a fraction of the alternative.
SOURCE: Hansard|https://hansard.parliament.uk/Lords/2026-10-26/debates/B413EFAE-E7D4-4742-986D-092884E0D8D1/CyberSecurityAndResilience(NetworkandInformationSystems)Bill

### The regulator of personal data turns to agents and keeps accountability with the deployer
The ICO's call for evidence on agentic AI runs until 20 November and covers data security, transparency, accountability, automated decision-making, fairness, purpose limitation and lawfulness, feeding the statutory code of practice required by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. It follows the foundation model report of 8 October, in which ten developers made or committed to changes and engagement with X.AI was paused pending an investigation into Grok. Richard Nevinson, director of technology regulation, said that an agent acting with autonomy "is not an excuse for poor compliance".
*Strategic implication:* where an agent reads student records or writes to a supplier system, the controller analysis, the minimisation argument and the audit trail belong to the university. A data protection impact assessment written for a chatbot will not cover an agent that takes actions, and the difference is worth a template of its own.
SOURCE: ICO|https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/ico-and-stakeholder-consultations/2026/10/agentic-ai-call-for-evidence/

### Microsoft's chief executive names control, knowledge and trust as the constraints
Speaking at the Microsoft Frontier Tour in London on 21 October, in an account published on 30 October, Satya Nadella set out three challenges for organisations adopting agentic AI: keeping humans in control of consequential actions, retaining ownership of organisational knowledge, and establishing trust in systems whose behaviour is becoming harder to predict. Governance sits above model capability in that framing.
*Strategic implication:* the vendor with the largest seat footprint in UK higher education has put institutional control ahead of model choice as the limiting factor. That gives a CTO grounds to spend the next budget round on instrumenting the estate rather than expanding it.
SOURCE: Technology Record|https://www.technologyrecord.com/article/trust-and-governance-will-define-the-next-phase-of-enterprise-ai-says-microsoft

### An investigation counts who was in the room when datacentre policy was written
Spotlight on Corruption's review of AI growth zones, reported on 27 October, found that ministers and senior officials met 137 businesses to discuss datacentre policy, two community groups and no environmental organisations, and that 38 senior officials moved into AI companies between July 2024 and September 2026, nine of them from the department that shaped AI strategy. The findings rest on 40 freedom of information requests to 18 agencies.
*Strategic implication:* the planning record for the physical infrastructure that AI depends on is now obtainable by a local authority, a residents' group or a student union. Any institution hosting or partnering on datacentre capacity should expect the consultation record to be read back at it.
SOURCE: The Guardian|https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/oct/27/uk-government-environmental-groups-ai-datacentres-expansion-plans-investigation

## 02 — Teaching & Learning

### Cambridge's scrutiny body finds the central guidance contradictory and vague
The Board of Scrutiny's assessment, published in the Cambridge University Reporter on 21 October, describes the university's central guidance on artificial intelligence as "sometimes contradictory and vague", and recommends annual AI inductions for students and staff along with the option of oral examinations where misuse is suspected. The formal position remains that unacknowledged AI content in a summative assessment is misconduct unless the brief permits it, whilst the permissible role of AI in unassessed learning stays undefined.
*Strategic implication:* the defect is structural. Policy written at the centre cannot resolve the question a tutor faces on a Tuesday, and module-level assessment categories published before enrolment do more work than another central statement.
SOURCE: Cambridge Reporter|https://www.admin.cam.ac.uk/reporter/2026-27/weekly/6840/section4.shtml

### ai@cam funds six teaching experiments and puts assessment design at the centre
The AI for Teaching and Learning initiative has selected projects in architecture, economics, genomic medicine, law, physics and psychology, each running six months on up to £10,000, with technical support from the AI Clinic. In the Algorithmic Trading Robot Design course, students build trading strategies and are assessed on how their robots behave in a simulated market under conditions they did not choose, with AI diagnostic feedback closing the loop. Neil Lawrence, who chairs ai@cam, said universities still have a great deal to learn about what AI means for the development of knowledge, expertise and independent judgement.
*Strategic implication:* the design pattern travels beyond Cambridge and beyond trading. Assess the behaviour of the artefact under unfamiliar conditions and the submission stops being a proxy for capability, which is the only durable answer to a student who can generate a polished output in ten minutes.
SOURCE: ai@cam|https://ai.cam.ac.uk/news/six-cambridge-projects-to-explore-how-ai-is-changing-teaching-and-learning

### A four-tier assessment classification spreads through the sector
Liverpool's guidance, in force since August 2026, places every assessment in one of four categories: AI prohibited, restricted, actively encouraged, or integral to the task. The tier is recorded per assessment in curriculum documentation, visible to a student before the brief arrives and auditable by a reviewer afterwards.
*Strategic implication:* a tier held in the curriculum record is the cheapest control available to an institution facing an OfS review or a student appeal, because it converts a judgement about intent into a record of a published rule.
SOURCE: The Tab|https://thetab.com/2026/10/16/is-this-the-downfall-of-turnitin-heres-what-you-need-to-know

### QAA's draft standards principles stay open for sector comment
The draft principles for assuring academic standards in the age of AI, published on 19 October, remain under consultation with a sector advisory group, and sit alongside QAA's finding that variance in practice runs through modules and tutors inside a single institution.
*Strategic implication:* the framework that matters will be the one published after consultation, and every reviewer will cite it. A response submitted now costs an afternoon and shapes the reference document for the next validation cycle.
SOURCE: QAA|https://www.qaa.ac.uk/news-events/news/draft-principles-for-assuring-academic-standards-in-the-age-of-ai

### The evidence base for teaching technology is being tested by the technology itself
Writing in Times Higher Education on 29 October, Sandra Leaton Gray argues that producing better evidence about interventions has never been the same thing as overcoming educational disadvantage, and that AI is exposing the limits of a "what works" framing. The argument arrives as institutions buy tutoring, feedback and analytics on vendor claims rather than controlled trials.
*Strategic implication:* the evidence standard belongs in the procurement, with a comparison group, a defined measure and a published result. Without it the teaching estate fills with tools whose effect nobody can demonstrate and whose renewal nobody can refuse.
SOURCE: Times Higher Education|https://www.timeshighereducation.com/opinion/ai-exposing-limits-educations-what-works-obsession

## 03 — Workforce & Skills

### A services firm puts 25,000 people behind one laboratory's products
Cognizant's Frontier Capability Group, announced on 30 October, aligns more than 25,000 professionals to delivering OpenAI products including Codex and ChatGPT, across AI-native software engineering, application modernisation and agentic customer operations. Its Application Modernizer covers the lifecycle from reverse-engineering mainframe code to generating cloud-native code, with engineers reviewing every consequential decision, and the firm is running the same methods through its own sales and service operations first. Oracle opened an equivalent services practice on 27 October covering use-case selection, agent development, evaluation, governance and cost optimisation.
*Strategic implication:* forward deployed engineering now arrives as an industrial supply line with a headcount larger than most UK universities. The strategic question for an institution is whether to rent that capability by the engagement or to hold two or three engineers who know the registry, the admissions system and the studio, and who remain after the contract ends.
SOURCE: Cognizant|https://news.cognizant.com/2026-10-30-Cognizant-commits-more-than-25%2C000-professionals-to-OpenAI-delivery-as-enterprises-move-frontier-AI-into-production

### Employers expect AI to remake the first rung and have redesigned almost none of it
Writing for HEPI on 26 October, the argument rests on the Institute of Student Employers' Student Development Survey 2026: 87 per cent of employers expect AI to reshape graduate and apprentice roles, and 43 per cent say their entry-level roles have already evolved without formal redesign. ISE's recruitment data puts graduate hiring down 8 per cent year on year with 140 applications per vacancy. Three decisions are proposed: universities define graduate-level judgement within each discipline, employers weigh the developmental value of junior work before automating it, and government uses the Early Careers Jobs Alliance to build a graduate formation model for AI-exposed sectors.
*Strategic implication:* the apprenticeship function of junior work used to be free and implicit in an employment contract. Once it is automated, somebody has to pay for it deliberately, and a university is the only body with the assessment machinery to certify judgement.
SOURCE: HEPI|https://hepi.ac.uk/2026/10/26/ai-is-raising-the-first-rung-who-will-turn-graduates-into-professionals

### The digital jobs plan buys entry routes and leaves the AI content thin
The Digital and Technologies Jobs Plan, published on 21 October, projects around 800,000 digital and technology roles to fill over the next decade and sets an ambition of roughly 200,000 entry-level opportunities including apprenticeships, at about 20,000 a year. TechFirst aims to reach a million young people with AI and technology learning by 2028, and partners including Microsoft, Cisco, IBM, Cognizant, Arm, Kainos, QinetiQ and AtkinsRéalis have committed more than 10,000 places. The plan also records that organisations hiring apprentices into AI roles rose from 3 per cent in 2020 to 19 per cent in 2025, that apprentices remain 3 per cent of the AI workforce and that 13 per cent of graduate schemes include AI training.
*Strategic implication:* the funding is aimed at volume routes into work. The gap it documents sits in graduate scheme design and in the AI content of degree programmes, which is where an institution's influence actually runs, and where a placement or degree apprenticeship partnership converts into recruitment.
SOURCE: DfE|https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/digital-and-technologies-jobs-plan/digital-and-technologies-jobs-plan

### Universities UK's roadmap names a trailblazer in every course
The Future Jobs Roadmap commits the sector to meaningful work-based learning for all undergraduates by 2035 with a 50 per cent milestone by 2030, AI tools accessible to every undergraduate and, in every course, a lecturer designated as an AI trailblazer. It also asks for a universal lifetime entitlement to careers support and for employer co-designed modular provision.
*Strategic implication:* one named member of staff per course is specific and testable, which also means it fails visibly. The role needs hours, training and a promotion route in the same academic year it is announced.
SOURCE: Universities UK|https://mmu.ac.uk/news-and-events/news/story/universities-and-employers-join-forces-tackle-entry-level-job-squeeze

### Architects expect the profession to shrink faster than their own practices
The RIBA AI Report 2026, drawn from more than 1,100 responses gathered in March and April, finds 59 per cent agreeing that AI adoption will lead to staff reductions across architecture against 30 per cent expecting reductions in their own organisation, with 9 per cent reporting cuts that have already happened. Sixty-one per cent agree that early-career professionals will find it harder to acquire essential skills, and 19 per cent of practices hold a documented AI policy.
*Strategic implication:* a school of architecture is being asked to defend the training route that practices believe they are closing. Placement design, live project work and an assessed audit of an agentic workflow become the substitute for the tasks that used to teach judgement, and they need to be timetabled rather than hoped for.
SOURCE: RIBA|https://www.riba.org/news/ai-adoption-reaches-tipping-point-across-uk-architecture/

## 04 — Policy & Operations

### Autodesk moves from the seat to the meter
AEC Magazine reported on 27 October that Autodesk began charging for its AEC Data Model API on 6 October, at one Flex token for every 5 MB of data exported beyond the included allowance. The same piece sets out the alternative now within reach of a practice: running its own model and orchestrator on hardware it controls, keeping the reasoning layer and corporate knowledge inside that boundary, and connecting agents to Revit, Rhino and Speckle through MCP.
*Strategic implication:* the unit of AI cost in a design workflow has become data movement. A faculty that models its budget on seat counts will undershoot, and one that keeps a per-project usage ledger can renegotiate from evidence when the renewal arrives.
SOURCE: AEC Magazine|https://aecmag.com/ai/autodesk-beyond-the-seat-the-meter/

### The agent-native BIM question moves from demonstration to procurement
AEC Magazine's September/October edition tests write access to Revit through Claude and MCP, profiles Motif as an agent-native BIM platform built against Revit's architecture, and examines tools that navigate building codes. Allplan's Max release on 28 October extends engineering AI across its portfolio.
*Strategic implication:* write access to a live model is the moment an agent can alter a coordinated design without a person approving the change. Approval gates and a readable change log have to land in the same term as the capability, and the test-on-a-copy discipline belongs in the teaching brief.
SOURCE: AEC Magazine|https://aecmag.com/magazines

### ServiceNow and OpenAI put agent actions behind the system of record
The expansion announced on 28 October places a ServiceNow plugin for ChatGPT in the OpenAI marketplace, so an agent can find related incidents, assess change risk and update records whilst ServiceNow's Action Fabric applies the customer's own permissions and business rules. Codex is planned for the AI Workflow Factory during 2027, a joint security product follows, and voice is expected in the first quarter of 2027.
*Strategic implication:* the pattern of the week is that agent authority is delegated to the system that already holds the rules. That is a cleaner architecture than standing access to everything the agent might touch, and it is the design to demand from any vendor pitching an agent for student records or finance.
SOURCE: OpenAI|https://www.openai.com/index/openai-servicenow-partnership-expansion/

### GitHub ships agent governance as a product default
At releases on 28 and 29 October, GitHub made Copilot Fusion generally available with model routing, cascades and critique workflows at administrator-controlled effort levels, made Agentic Workflows generally available so Markdown descriptions compile into Actions YAML with read-only permissions by default, and put the maximum review-effort level of Copilot code review into public preview. Copilot can also be included with GitHub Enterprise for every licensed user, metered against the enterprise budget. IBM and HashiCorp made Agent Identity and Vault 2.2 generally available in the same week, recording who owns an agent and limiting what it can reach.
*Strategic implication:* read-only by default, constrained model lists and named agent owners are the three controls a research group needs before it runs agents against a shared repository. Each is now configuration, which removes the excuse for leaving it to goodwill.
SOURCE: GitHub|https://github.blog/changelog/

### A cheaper frontier model moves the cost into verification
GPT-6.1 Terra arrived on 26 October at $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output, with computer use reported at parity with the top tier at roughly a tenth of the token price, a marked fall in error rates on adversarial prompts and 50.3 per cent on Terminal-Bench 4.0 at maximum effort. Liquid AI shipped six 2B-class LFM3 models on 27 October, and Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro opened at $2 and $10 with weights promised by 4 November.
*Strategic implication:* when a competent agent costs a tenth of last quarter's price, deployment stops being the constraint and checking becomes the scarce resource. Budget should shift from inference towards evaluation harnesses, logging and the human review hours that make an agent's output defensible.
SOURCE: OpenAI|https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-1-terra/

## What this means for your institution

1. **Attach the Jisc six areas to every AI renewal before signature.** Demand written answers on service entitlements, pricing structure, usage metrics, charging mechanics, model-version continuity and exit. A supplier that will not commit in writing has given you a finding for the board, and a supplier that will has given you a contract you can administer.

2. **Build a usage ledger you can query without asking anyone.** Meter consumption by department, by workflow and by agent, and translate it into cost per enrolment, per assessment cycle and per studio project. Jisc's demand for visibility is only useful to an institution that already measures its own side of the meter.

3. **Treat agent permissions as delegated authority with named holders.** For each agent that can write, record the owner, the systems it reaches, the actions that require a human approval and the log it writes to. Amendment 70 gives ministers powers over networks compromised through loss of control of an agent, and the institution that can produce that register answers an inspector in an hour.

4. **Rebuild the first rung inside the curriculum.** Where a task used to train a junior, write an assessed substitute: a supervised brief with unfamiliar conditions, an audit of what the agent did and a defence of the decision. That is the artefact employers paying for judgement will accept, and it is what the Cambridge and Liverpool examples already demonstrate.

## Worth watching
